# MACRO authorship and credit policy — DRAFT

> **Status: DRAFT for discussion. Not agreed. Not in force.**
> Prepared 2026-10-03 for James Wetzel to take to the MACRO consortium, in response to
> finding ED.E4 of the plan review (`committee/reviews/2026-10-03/journal-editor.md`): every
> manuscript skeleton in this repository carries one author and a placeholder ORCID, for
> frames taken by a five-college consortium over three years, and no policy says who else
> belongs on the byline. Nothing here binds anyone until the consortium adopts it. Every
> number in square brackets is a proposal to be argued over, not a decision.

## 1. Scope

This policy covers every paper, research note, telegram, data release and conference
contribution that uses frames from the Robert L. Mutel Telescope (RLMT) archive or the
pre-2023 Iowa archive, or products of the analysis pipeline in this repository.

## 2. Who is an author

A person is an author if they did **at least one** thing in column A **and** everything in
column B.

| A — a contribution to this paper | B — responsibilities every author accepts |
|---|---|
| Proposed or designed the observing programme the paper's data come from | Read the full draft and had the chance to comment before submission |
| Took, scheduled or supervised a substantial part of the paper's frames ([≥ 10 %] of the frames used, or [≥ 3] nights) | Can explain the paper's main result and their own part in it |
| Built or maintained a part of the telescope, camera, software or pipeline that the paper's result depends on | Agrees to be named, and to the author order |
| Carried out part of the reduction or analysis | Has a real ORCID and a current affiliation on file |
| Wrote or substantially revised the text or figures | |

Funding, general supervision, or membership of the consortium alone do not make an
author; they are acknowledged (§5). Whoever built and runs the instrument is an author on
the instrument paper and on any paper that could not exist without a specific piece of
their work, and is acknowledged elsewhere.

## 3. Student observers

Much of the archive was taken by undergraduates, often inside a course. The filename
prefix and the `OBSERVER` card record who scheduled each frame, and the pipeline can list,
for any paper, every observer code with the number of frames it contributed.

- A student who meets §2 is an author, with the affiliation they held when they did the
  work, whether or not they are still at that college.
- A student who took frames used by a paper but does not meet the §2 threshold is named
  in the acknowledgements, by name, unless they opt out.
- Faculty supervisors are responsible for finding former students and for telling the
  lead author who they are; the lead author is responsible for asking. An observer code
  nobody can attach to a person is listed as a code in the data release, not silently
  dropped.

## 4. Author order

1. **Lead author** — the person who did most of the analysis and writing.
2. **Tier 1, in order of contribution** — the people without whom this particular paper
   would not exist (typically two to five). The lead author proposes the order.
3. **Tier 2, alphabetical** — everyone else who meets §2, students included.
4. An optional final group author, "the MACRO Consortium", if the consortium wants one.

Each paper carries a contribution statement (CRediT roles) so that alphabetical order does
not hide who did what.

## 5. Acknowledgements and standard text

Every paper acknowledges, in an agreed standard paragraph: the MACRO Consortium and its
member institutions; Winer Observatory and its staff; the telescope's namesake and
funding sources; observers below the authorship threshold (§3); and the data and software
it used. [The standard paragraph is to be written and attached to this policy.]

## 6. The opt-in window

1. When a paper has a complete draft, the lead author circulates the abstract, the
   proposed author list and the script-generated observer table to the consortium list.
2. Anyone who believes they qualify under §2 says so within **[21] days**; anyone listed
   may decline within the same window.
3. The full draft goes to all authors at least **[14] days** before submission. Silence
   after two reminders is treated as "no comment", not as consent to be listed: an author
   who never answers is moved to the acknowledgements and told so.
4. **Time-critical papers** (a T CrB eruption telegram or letter): the window is **[48]
   hours** for a telegram and **[7] days** for a letter, and the pre-agreed eruption
   author list (to be drawn up now, under this policy, before the eruption) applies.

## 7. Programmes with a principal investigator

Where frames were taken for a programme with an identifiable PI (for example the 2023
dwarf-galaxy candidate imaging), that PI is contacted **before** analysis is drafted, is
offered authorship at Tier 1 or the lead, and may ask for a reasonable embargo
([6] months proposed) on results that overlap work they have in progress. Archival use
of another member's programme without contact is not acceptable under this policy.

## 8. Data, software and identifiers

- Every paper has a data-availability and a software-availability statement, and a DOI
  (Zenodo) for the databases and code release it was built from.
- Every author supplies an ORCID before submission; no placeholder identifiers.
- Data releases list all observer codes with frame counts, whether or not the observers
  are authors.

## 9. Disagreements

A dispute about inclusion or order goes first to the lead author and the faculty
representatives of the institutions involved, then to the consortium's governing group,
whose decision is final. A paper is not submitted while a dispute about its author list is
open.

## 10. Questions the consortium has to answer before this can be adopted

1. Is the §2 threshold right — [10 %] of frames or [3] nights — or should any contributing
   observer be an author?
2. Does the consortium want a group author (§4.4), and on which papers?
3. Who maintains the list linking observer codes to people, and may the names be published
   in a data release?
4. Does the policy apply to the pre-2023 Iowa archive, and who speaks for those observers?
5. Who is the standing contact for instrument authorship at Winer and at the University
   of Iowa?
6. Is an embargo (§7) acceptable to the membership, and for how long?
7. Use of AI tools in analysis and writing: the journals require disclosure. The
   consortium should decide what its standard disclosure sentence says.
