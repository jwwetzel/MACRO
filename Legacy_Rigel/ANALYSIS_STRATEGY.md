# Legacy archive (2015–2022) — analysis strategy (short form)
**Status: GO-CANDIDATE, not a commitment.** Whether a sixth project opens is James's decision, and it must not compete with T CrB for effort. Every number cited here is on `docs/Legacy_Rigel/legacy_census.html`.

**Question (pre-registered Q1):** do the eclipsing systems with minima in ≥3 seasons show period change or eclipse-timing variations over 2017–2022? The census found these are HW Vir-type sdB + dM binaries (section 8b), a crowded field.

**Reader:** eclipse-timing observers of these systems. **Venue:** JAAVSO or OEJV; a data note if Step 0 fails.

**Steps:**
0. **Novelty check first, and alone.** List published and archived minima for 2017–2022. If they already cover our seasons at equal or better precision, the outcome is a table of minima in a data note.
1. **An independent clock per season.** Section 5b times these same stars, so their eclipses can't calibrate their own clock. Use archived exoplanet transits from the same seasons (S3b method); seasons without one give relative timings only.
2. **Timings.** Use S3b's fit unchanged, with each camera epoch's stamp convention (the AC4040 is mid-exposure). Report the signed injection bias.
3. **O−C.** Compare against TESS-anchored and published ephemerides side by side. Report χ²ν per season with its dof, and every null with the amplitude it could have detected.

**Excluded:** new observing, absolute photometry (no calibration frames exist), and the 2023 frames (they are RLMT exposures).

**Owed in either case:** the census database and a data note go to Zenodo.
