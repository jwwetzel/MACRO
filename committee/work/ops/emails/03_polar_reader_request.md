Dear Dr. ____________________,

I am writing to ask a favour of someone who works on polars and has no connection to our
group: would you be willing to read a draft before we submit it, and tell us in a sentence
or two what you think it shows?

The paper comes from the MACRO consortium's 0.5 m Robert L. Mutel Telescope at Winer
Observatory (five undergraduate-serving institutions; I am at Coe College). Its content:

- **ST LMi**, cycle-resolved g, r, i photometry over two instrument eras about a year
  apart. The main result is the colour through the bright phase: g − r changes by roughly
  0.75 mag across the orbit, with the same morphology in both eras, tagged by accretion
  state from the survey record.
- A bright-phase timing analysis over a 1.9-year baseline that yields a limit on any
  change in the spot longitude or period, not a detection.
- A short section on **YZ Cnc** (a superhump search that sets an amplitude limit, and
  flickering statistics).
- A coverage audit of VV Pup, EU UMa and AN UMa, and a public release of the light curves
  and the databases every number in the paper is generated from.

The draft is being revised after an internal review and will be about 18 pages, aimed at
AJ. I expect to have a version worth your time by ____________________.

What I am asking for is small and specific:

1. After reading it, write down in one or two sentences what you take the result to be.
   If your summary and ours differ, the paper is not yet clear, and that is the thing we
   most need to know.
2. Tell us what we have missed in the literature. The internal review found the
   comparison with previous work thin, and we would rather hear it from you than from a
   referee.
3. Anything that made you stop believing a figure.

I am not asking for a referee report, and there is no deadline beyond whatever suits you;
two or three weeks would be ideal. You would be thanked in the acknowledgements unless
you prefer otherwise, and if you would rather not, a one-line "no" is entirely fine.

With thanks for considering it,
