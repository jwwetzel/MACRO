1. **Factual error.** The abstract says "The window covers 1.09 orbital cycles". It covers 0.55 orbital cycles (phase 0.292 -> 0.837, Munari P = 227.55 d), which is 1.09 *ellipsoidal* cycles (NOVELTY_TABLE.md:156). Correct the sentence.
2. **Merge Fig 6 into Fig 1.** Within 0.55 of a cycle, orbital phase is a linear rescaling of time, so EW against phase repeats Fig 1. Add a top orbital-phase axis (ellipsoidal extrema marked) and a line-flux panel to Fig 1. That leaves five figures.
3. **Lead with the result.** Put the ARAS agreement and the novelty claim, quantified, in the first two sentences. The novelty claim: [N] dates of one instrument over 124 d, against 49 for the densest single ARAS observer (JRF/MTP-US, NOVELTY_TABLE.md sec. 3) and 97 for ARAS pooled. That comparison is the only defensible "what is new"; "ARAS is denser" alone undersells it.
4. **Replace internal language.** Change "pass a pixel-based identity gate" to "are confirmed from the pixels to contain the T CrB spectrum".
5. **Cut the archival-imaging sentences** to one clause pointing to the two tables ("archival imaging gives B-band anchors and flickering limits, Tables N-M"). Drop "R and I are clipped".
6. **Title.** "2025 Pre-Eruption Dip and Recovery" is a claim the abstract never makes, and there are two grisms, not one. Either the abstract states what the B curve shows across the window, or the title becomes something like: "Hα Equivalent Widths of T CrB from Two Slitless Grisms on a 0.5 m Telescope, Validated against ARAS: 2025 February-June".
