Cataclysmic-Variable Time Series — the case

Single-night, cycle-resolved, accretion-state-tagged multi-colour light curves of three polars (ST LMi, VV Pup, EU UMa) and the SU UMa dwarf nova YZ Cnc — the per-cycle quasi-simultaneous colour coverage that phase-averaged survey folds cannot produce for 90–125 min binaries — plus bright-phase timing read as an accretion-spot longitude tracker against 40-year literature ephemerides, and YZ Cnc's dense 2024 season analysed for superhumps if AAVSO confirms the runs were in outburst. Colour science is two independent within-era analyses, never a stitched series across the 2024-05 instrument seam. The full machine-readable light curves ship as a data product.

Where it is going: ApJ (AASTeX 7 skeleton in manuscripts/CV_TimeSeries/main.tex)

Q2

Are the images good enough — and what does “good enough” mean?

CV-S5: STALE

Seeing, sky brightness, transparency, airmass and the Moon all vary across this archive. A cut has to be made somewhere — but a cut chosen by taste is a knob a referee can turn. What does each quality axis actually cost, in magnitudes of scatter?

The deciding number
86.2% usable
The decision it produced
Decision. Keep a frame when its seeing is ≤ 3.88″, its sky no more than 3.38× its series median, its ensemble zero point no more than 0.325 mag below the night's median (the transparency / cloud test), and its airmass at or below X = 2.40. That last cut is new and small (42 frames): the bound the data support is “no measurable degradation below X = 2.4”, not “airmass never matters”, and the observed range reaches X = 2.95. Registration method gets no threshold: inside a depth bin the translation vote is never the worst route and is often the best, so its 613 frames are validated rather than merely used. Altogether 6,706 of 7,780 frames (86.2%) are usable.

Q3

What is the noise floor, and is it what the detector says it should be?

CV-S5: STALE

Photon statistics predict a scatter that falls as stars get brighter. Real photometry stops improving somewhere. Where, and what is the flat part made of? The answer sets every detection limit downstream, so it has to be measured rather than asserted — and the S2 detector work gives us the pieces to predict what it should be.

The deciding number
floor 4– 26 mmag
The decision it produced
Decision. The systematic floor is 4–26 mmag depending on series, and it is 20× larger than scintillation (median). It is therefore instrumental — flat-field residual, ensemble zero-point error, and low-level variability of the “constant” stars — not atmospheric, and in principle improvable. The photon model is close to right on most series: the fitted scaling k has median 1.15 over all 28 series, and 22 of 28 fall inside the range the gain bracket allows. The 6 that do not are named in the table and are a model failure, not an average to be taken: euuma|e76|r (1.69), vvpup|e76|r (1.70), vvpup|e72|i (1.77), euuma|e76|g (1.99), vvpup|e76|g (2.92), vvpup|e76|i (24.68). Note also what the gain bracket is and is not worth here: swinging the gain across its entire 3.0× range moves k by a median of only 19%, because the fit takes its leverage from the faint end where sky and read noise dominate — so the bracket is propagated honestly but it is not the dominant uncertainty in the prediction. Averaging is not free: 31 of 92 ladders have a slope above their own 95th-percentile white null and 27 exceed it on the red-noise factor, median penalty 1.32× at a median 0.37 Porb. The median slope (-0.37) is inside the null band, so it is the per-ladder tests and not the median that carry this conclusion.

Q4

What periods can this sampling resolve at all?

CV-S5: STALE

Precision is worthless at a period the sampling cannot distinguish from its alias. Before any periodogram is read, the sampling's own transform — the spectral window |Σ e−2πift|2/N2 — has to be looked at, because every peak in a real periodogram is the truth convolved with it.

The deciding number
worst resolved ±1 c/d alias: 0.93 of the window
The decision it produced
Decision. On every multi-night set the ±1 c/d aliases of the orbital frequency are fully resolved (resolution << 1 c/d) and carry up to 0.84 of the window power on the solar-day comb — the one that matters for ground-based scheduling — rising to 0.93 if the sidereal comb is included as well. That is the sampling's capacity to manufacture an alias. Whether the alias actually wins is a different question, it depends on amplitude, and this page measures it directly in §4 rather than inferring it here: at the amplitudes these targets really show, the tallest peak lands on the true frequency in essentially every injection. So: the alias risk is real near the detection threshold and negligible at the real modulation amplitudes. A single night's peak, by contrast, is 3–9 c/d wide — it cannot separate the aliases either, but for the opposite reason: there is only one peak. Published periodograms must carry the window, the solar-day alias power and the measured misidentification rate beside them.

Q5

What amplitude can we actually detect?

CV-S5: STALE

“We measure 25 mmag scatter” is not a science statement. “We can detect a 30 mmag modulation at 1.9 h” is. Converting one into the other requires the real timestamps (so the window enters), the real noise (so correlated systematics enter) and a false-alarm threshold measured the same way (so a red archive cannot borrow a white threshold).

The deciding number
injection through real timestamps & real noise
The decision it produced
Decision. Quote the known-period contour as this paper's detection limit — the orbital periods are literature values with decades of baseline, so nothing here is measuring them from scratch. On that footing the measured A90 at each target's own period differs from the analytic σ√(4z/N) the strategy document uses by a factor of 0.5–2.8 — and note that range spans 1 in both directions. The formula is conservative at a known period, because its z charges a blind-search look-elsewhere penalty this paper does not owe; it is optimistic when a period genuinely has to be found, where the measured limit is 1.5–4.9× worse than the formula on the multi-night sets. One expression that errs by two to five times in opposite directions depending on which question is asked is not a detection limit: it assumes white noise, an alias-free window and one fixed z, and this archive supplies none of the three. What must not be quoted at all is the single-night period-determination contour (6.0–19.3×) as though it were a detection limit — those rows, greyed above, measure how few cycles one night holds. Every detection limit in the manuscript must be the injection number under a named score mode, not the formula. Per-night detrending buys a factor 0.96–1.28 at the orbital period (median 1.16) — measured, not assumed. And on alias confusion the measurement is reassuring: at the amplitudes these polars actually show, the tallest peak lands on the true frequency in essentially every trial.

Q6

How precisely can one bright-phase edge be timed?

CV-S5: STALE

The strategy document's Q2 rests on a threshold it explicitly refuses to assume: per-cycle epoch precision σt < 60 s, to be demonstrated by injection before adoption (§4.16), with a pre-defined fallback to seasonal means (§6.10a). This section runs that demonstration.

The deciding number
per-cycle σt 2–51 s
The decision it produced
Decision. On the richest night, with the edge shape known exactly, per-cycle σt is 1.8–26 s — the 60 s threshold is met. With the shape and depth assumed wrong it rises to 14.3–51 s. On the median-density night, which is what the tier will mostly be built from, the same two answers are 2.3–150 s and 17.4–144 s. On ST LMi, the flagship, the mismatched answer is 47–50 s on its best night and 71–76 s on a typical one. Night-mean timings are 1.4–8.5 s. The per-cycle O–C tier survives on the best nights, with almost no margin, and only if the paper carries an explicit edge-shape systematic of order 50 s beside every per-cycle timing — and it must be sized on the median night, not the demonstration night.

Q14

Three attacks on our own answer

CV-S6: STALE_UPSTREAM
The decision it produced
Era 76 (Mode0, server_reduced pixels) carries a radial residual of -71.0 mmag from centre to corner, significant in 11 of its 12 blocks across 4 independent fields. Every other era averages 11.5 mmag. A systematic that repeats across four unrelated star fields, in one camera era and one pixel provenance and not in the others, is not a property of any field: it is a property of the reduction that made those pixels. The signature is an illumination-correction error, i.e. a flat field that does not describe the true vignetting — and the SIGN of the effect is what picks that explanation out from its rivals. The residual falls with radius: stars near the edge measure BRIGHTER than the catalogue says, stars in the middle fainter. A degrading point-spread function toward the field edge, the other obvious candidate, would push the opposite way (light spilling out of a fixed 4-arcsec aperture makes edge stars measure FAINTER), so it cannot be the cause. An over-corrected vignetting profile — dividing by a flat that is too low at the edges — produces exactly this sign. Note WHICH eras show it: the ones reduced by the observatory's own pipeline (server_reduced). The eras calibrated here from staged era-matched masters (local_master) do not. That is a falsifiable claim and the cheapest way to test it is to re-reduce one era-76 night from raw with local masters and re-run this stage on it.

Q23

Night by night, and what backs each one

CV-S7: STALE_UPSTREAM
The decision it produced
The Feb 21–24 block — our densest, 819 frames — has no independent coverage at all. It is also the block whose state matters most. So the verdict there does not rest on our own numbers: it rests on a physical bracket. The independent record has 2024-02-20 at V=13.85 and 2024-02-25 at V=13.63, five days apart. An SU UMa superoutburst holds a plateau for 8 days or more. A superoutburst cannot begin, peak at V≈11 and return to V=13.6 inside a five-day gap — so no superoutburst occurred on those nights, and that conclusion survives deleting every RLMT row from the AAVSO record. Our own photometry then adds the detail: a normal outburst, quiescent on Feb 21, rising ~1.2 mag on Feb 22, peaking V≈12.8 on Feb 23, fading Feb 24 and back near quiescence by Feb 28.

The decision it produced

Q32

Faint-phase forced photometry and upper limits

CV-S8: STALE_UPSTREAM

A polar in a low state drops below detection. What does dropping those epochs do to every statistic computed afterwards?

The deciding number
CV-P2-faint-limits
The decision it produced
The gate. A block that cannot demonstrate closure — at least 10 frames with a detected target, closing to a median of 1 px or better — does not publish limits. This is not a formality. The first production run produced 66 “forced detections” of EU UMa in the merged 2026 Fast block, whose frame transforms closed to 645–1,650 px on 87 of 153 attempts and whose measured signal-to-noise alternated between 3 and 55 frame to frame — an aperture landing on a bright neighbour on half the frames. That block has never detected its target, so there is no frame on which its forced position can be checked, and a limit measured at an unverifiable position is not a limit, it is a number.

Q37

O−C construction and cycle-count analysis

CV-S9: STALE_UPSTREAM

An O−C diagram plots an observed epoch minus the epoch a linear ephemeris predicts, and that subtraction needs an integer: which cycle is this? Between the VSX zero point and our nights there are tens of thousands of cycles, and an error in the period accumulates over every one of them. Once the accumulated drift reaches half a cycle the integer is no longer determined, and an O−C computed on the wrong one is not a noisy result — it is a fabricated one, and it looks exactly like a real period change.
