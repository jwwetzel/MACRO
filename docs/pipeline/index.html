The decision it produced
The reduced tree is derived data: no reduced file is ever a canonical science frame, and raw_reduced_links is the only bridge between the two populations. Q14

The coverage matrix

S0b: STALE_UPSTREAM

For each camera era with science frames, which calibration requirements are actually met — to spec, from raw frames?

The deciding number
per era: bias · dark per exposure · flat per filter
The decision it produced
A dark matches a science exposure when |Δt| ≤ max(0.02 s, 0.5% · t) — exact-match policy with a tolerance that absorbs driver float fuzz (∼10−5 relative) and can never bridge two real exposure settings (the archive ladder steps by ≥25%). Specs count RAW frames only (≥20 bias, ≥15 darks per exposure, ≥10 flats per filter); master products satisfy nothing but are tallied (master_only status) because they remain usable. Scaled-dark fallback is noted only where an era has bias frames (eras: 47, 72, 76) — without a bias, dark scaling is arithmetic fiction. Science FILTER strings that collide with the calibration vocabulary (‘dark’/‘bias’/‘flat’ — filter-wheel/header glitches passed through verbatim) keep their matrix cells (1 cell(s), 83 science frames) but are excluded from the shopping list: a flat “in filter dark” is not an acquirable item.

Q21

The ceiling memo: where every mode's scale actually ends

S2: STALE_UPSTREAM

The SN panel measured the High Gain clip at ~3,530–3,550 ADU; the CV team adopted 3,500 with a 3,200 veto (external priors, quoted from the facility notes as documented constants). Which number is right, is the detector 12-bit, and what are the ceilings of the OTHER seven modes nobody measured?

The decision it produced
Adopted per-mode ceilings and vetoes as tabled above: High Gain 3,496 ADU (veto 3,200), StackPro 56,062 (= 16.04 × the single-read clip), Mode0/Fast/blank-2026 at the full 16-bit code 65,535 (veto 60,200), iKon 1 MHz at 64,674. The GSENSE4040 High Gain scale is 12-bit-consistent: the mound at 3,496 fits in 12 bits (212 = 4,096) and leaves 599 codes of the 16-bit FITS container unused — but a per-pixel mound spread over tens of ADU cannot distinguish a 12-bit digitization clip (which lands on ONE shared code, as Mode0/Fast do at 65,535) from per-pixel analog full-well saturation or a clamp below 4,096, so “confirmed” waits for the vendor ADC readback (October). The veto rule is floor(0.92 × ceiling) to 100 ADU; against the CV team's prior veto of 3,200, the measured 3,200 reproduces it exactly.

Q24

Linearity from archival exposure ladders

S2: STALE_UPSTREAM

Does flux scale with exposure time — and can the 2024-05-20 Vega BeStar ladder (0.0001–0.1 s) calibrate the short end?

The decision it produced
Adopted per-mode linearity bounds as tabled: best-witnessed modes (Mode0, Fast, iKon 1 MHz) are linear to ≤1.4% over their witnessed ranges; the High Gain family carries a ≤4.2% bound that is sky/cloud-limited, not detector-limited — treat it as an upper limit, not a measured non-linearity. Q33

Label vs measurement: the gate correction

S1: STALE_UPSTREAM

Astrometry cannot apply to a slitless spectrum, so the candidate universe must exclude spectra. Earlier versions of this page decided which frames were spectra by reading the FILTER string and matching it against five known grism names (grism, hagrism, hrg, lrg, oggrism). Stage S2c has since measured dispersion per frame — parallel traces along a common grating axis. Do the label and the measurement agree about what a spectrum is?

The deciding number
a published rate, taxonomy and verdict were computed over the wrong denominator
The decision it produced
A frame is excluded as a spectrum when, and only when, S2c MEASURED it dispersed. The FILTER label no longer gates anything; it survives in the code (is_solvable_candidate_by_label) purely to compute the table above. The rule for each of the four dispersion classes, stated so that none of them defaults silently:
  • dispersed → EXCLUDED. Measured traces on a common grating axis. It is a spectrum.
  • direct → INCLUDED, whatever the FILTER says. 178 frames carrying a grism-looking label are measured direct images. A label loses to a measurement of the same fact; these return to the universe.
  • indeterminate → INCLUDED. S2c looked and could not certify either way. This moves 960 frames in (the indeterminate frames that carried a grism-looking label; the rest were already in). The reason is that the gate's question is ontological — is this an image? — never predictive — will it solve?. Excluding frames because they look unlikely to solve would inflate the very rate this experiment measures. S2c's commonest indeterminate reason is literally “no usable sources extracted”, which describes a blank IMAGE far more often than a spectrum — and the production batch corroborates it (below).
  • unmeasured → INCLUDED. S2c targeted the dispersion-suspect population, so 58,599 candidates were never measured at all. This rule moves zero frames — every unmeasured frame carries an ordinary label and was already in — but it still has to be stated, because the alternative (exclude anything not certified direct) would delete most of the backlog on no evidence whatever.
In one sentence: exclusion must be earned by a positive measurement. The old label rule and its mirror image (“exclude whatever is not certified direct”) share one flaw — removing frames from a denominator without evidence.

Q40

Clock validation: AG LMi as the standard

S3: STALE_UPSTREAM

Header consistency (sections 1-3) proves the stamps agree with EACH OTHER — not that the acquisition computer's clock agrees with UTC. Does an external astrophysical clock confirm the time axis?

The deciding number
an astrophysical event checks the wall clock
The decision it produced
The eclipse arrives at O−C = -294 ± 59 s against the VSX ephemeris. The absolute observatory clock is therefore consistent with zero error at the |Δt| < 4517 s (75 min) level — a ceiling set almost entirely by how badly AG LMi's own ephemeris is known, not by our measurement. Of that 4517 s, 294 s is the measured offset, 59 s its fit error, and 4165 s the ephemeris term. That last number propagates the period uncertainty from the independent Gaia DR3 eclipsing-binary solution (1.3590118 d ±3.01e-05, Gaia DR3 vari_eclipsing_binary via Vizier I/358/veb, recorded 2026-08-18) over the 1584.0 cycles since the epoch. Propagating VSX's QUOTED LAST DIGIT instead would give only 50 s and a bound roughly eight times tighter — a printer's precision, not a measurement's, and this page will not quote it. The same Gaia-vs-VSX period difference predicts an O−C of -797 s at our epoch with a ±4121 s envelope, inside which our measurement sits comfortably: the offset belongs to the EPHEMERIS, not to the clock. What this test rules out is gross error — a wrong time zone, a missing leap second, a mis-set clock — and nothing finer.

Q42

Star matching without a WCS

S4: STALE

Most polar frames are unsolved (73–95% of the Sloan series carry pltsolvd=0 — S1’s future business). Can stars be matched across thousands of frames using GEOMETRY alone?

The deciding number
astroalign triangles → one reference per (target, era)
The decision it produced
Each (target, era) series is registered to ONE reference frame — chosen as the most-populated frame within the sharpest 75% of the series, PROVIDED it passes double-image quality control — by astroalign triangle matching, followed by one-to-one nearest-neighbour assignment with a seeing-scaled tolerance (max(2 px, 0.8 × ref FWHM)). Quality control exists because a guiding jump doubles every star into an equal-brightness pair and thereby INFLATES the detection count — the very statistic the selection rewards: a candidate is rejected when more than 20% of its stars have a companion within 3 × FWHM at a flux ratio under 2 (the paired-star fraction and rejection count are tabulated above — clean frames sit near zero, doubled frames near one). The star catalog IS the reference frame’s detection list; star identity is its detection id — stable, auditable, and WCS-free. astroalign’s RANSAC is seeded per frame (seeded_ransac), so every transform and star identity reproduces bit-for-bit on re-run. Frames that cannot produce 8 detections or defeat the triangle fit are marked failed_match, counted, and excluded — never force-fitted.

Q43

The Honeycutt ensemble

S4: STALE

With no photometric nights guaranteed and clouds in the data, how does every frame get a zero point it can defend?

The deciding number
robust ZP per frame + mean mag per star, per (target, era, filter)
The decision it produced
Honeycutt’s (1992) inhomogeneous ensemble, solved by alternating weighted least squares with a 4σ robust re-solve, a 5 mmag systematic weight floor, and the mean-ZP gauge. Comparison stars are chosen by stability iteration — candidates must appear on ≥50% of frames, and stars whose residual RMS exceeds max(2.5 × median, 20 mmag) are dropped each pass; the TARGET is excluded from comp duty a priori (a polar’s modulation must never set the zero point). 4 stable stars nearest the target’s magnitude are held OUT of the solve as check stars — their statistics below are honest hold-outs, not self-graded homework.

Q45

The empirical error model (S5 seed)

S4: STALE

Are the formal error bars TRUE? Both targets are polars whose modulation is signal, so the question is answered entirely by field CHECK stars held out of the ensemble solve.

The deciding number
check stars only — the target never grades its own errors
The decision it produced
Three statistics form the standard validation set every downstream paper reuses: (1) check-star RMS vs magnitude against the photon-noise floor; (2) the error INFLATION FACTOR sqrt(median χ²ν) of constant-star fits; (3) the Allan deviation of one long night, whose departure from τ−1/2 probes the correlated noise that averaging cannot remove. Three fine-print clauses S5 must honor: the χ² denominators are the formal errors WITH the 5 mmag systematic floor added in quadrature, so the factor calibrates floored errors, not raw photon errors; application is ONE-SIDED — use max(1, inflation), because a factor below 1 (see era 76 r) licenses no shrinking of error bars; and the check population passed the same stability screen as comps before being held out (RMS below max(2.5 × median comp RMS, 20 mmag)), so the check floor is measured on pre-selected quiet stars — with only 4 checks per series (era-76 rows pool the sparse AN UMa field with the crowded b ≈ +2° VV Pup field, 4 + 4), the tabulated medians are coarse. The iKon era (72) writes EGAIN=0, so its predicted errors omit the source shot term and its inflation factor is expected >1 by construction — the empirical model absorbs exactly this kind of header poverty, which is why S5 builds on measured scatter, never on claimed gains.
