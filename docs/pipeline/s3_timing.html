1 · What instant does the header stamp?

JD = DATE-OBS = UTC exposure start — proven, not assumed

Question

Every MACRO paper folds its data on a period; a half-exposure ambiguity in the time stamp would smear or shift every phase. The headers offer JD, DATE-OBS, and JD-HELIO — do they agree, and which instant of the exposure do they name?

Decision

Header JD (≡ DATE-OBS) is the UTC exposure START in every era family; S3 computes mid-exposure BJD_TDB from it from scratch. Header JD-HELIO is never used — it is heliocentric-only (up to ~4 s from barycentric), UTC-scale (~69 s from TDB), computed by uncontrolled acquisition-software approximations, and demonstrably garbage in the HDR family. The one re-stamped reduced-tree JD reinforces the S0 rule: timing always comes from the canonical (raw) copy.

Consequence

The era-audit table above is the paper appendix: any referee asking “how do you know your clocks?” gets pointed at 198,289 internal comparisons, 38 independent heliocentric recomputations, and the TELUT cross-check — all regenerable by one script.

2 · StackPro: the midpoint of a 16-fold sum

worst case 0.12 s, bounded by the data

Question

A StackPro frame is the on-camera SUM of 16 sub-reads (S2's PTC result). Header EXPTIME could mean the total span or the per-sub-read time, and any dead time between sub-reads would shift the photon-weighted midpoint. What is the mid-time of 20,375 StackPro science frames, and what is the worst case if the contiguity assumption is wrong?

Three independent facts pin the semantics: (1) across 15,007 measured StackPro series gaps the frame's wall-clock span never exceeds EXPTIME + 0.24 s (the minimum overhead, from the 0.5 s series) — so ALL internal dead time together is ≤ 0.24 s; (2) the sub-read gap is a fixed sensor property, independent of exposure length, so the bound from the shortest series applies to every StackPro frame; (3) MaxIm's own JD-HELIO on a 1024 s StackPro frame equals start + EXPTIME/2 to 0.13 s — the acquisition software also treats EXPTIME as the contiguous total.

Decision

StackPro mid-time = start + EXPTIME/2, under the stated assumption that the 16 sub-reads are contiguous and span EXPTIME. Worst case if dead time hides between sub-reads: the true midpoint shifts late by at most 0.12 s (half the measured 0.24 s dead-time ceiling) — recorded as stackpro_sum_midpoint_half_exptime in frame_times.mid_method so any user can query which frames carry it.

Consequence

CV eclipse timing on StackPro series (AG LMi 2023, the 1024 s deep fields) inherits a ≤ 0.12 s systematic — below the sub-second target and far below any eclipse-fit precision. Should a future camera manual contradict the contiguity assumption, only this constant changes and one re-run repairs every product.

3 · frame_times: BJD_TDB for every canonical science frame

192,120 of 193,550 frames on the shared time axis

Question

Every downstream product needs one authoritative mid-exposure BJD_TDB per frame — computed once, from scratch, with its inputs recorded, so no paper ever re-derives (or worse, half-derives) its own time axis.

Decision

The frame_times table (one row per canonical science frame, keyed by path) is the portfolio's time axis: bjd_tdb = UTC start + EXPTIME/2 → TDB scale → + barycentric light travel toward the frame's cataloged sky position, ephemeris de440s, Winer EarthLocation (31.6656°, -110.6018°, 1515 m). Frames without JD or coordinates keep a row with a NULL BJD and an explanatory method — nothing silently falls off the axis.

Consequence

One stated caveat travels with the table: the correction points at the FRAME CENTER. An object at a frame corner differs by up to ~1.3 s (26′ × 499 s/rad); a paper needing sub-second absolutes for an off-center object recomputes with macro_core.timing.bjd_tdb_from_utc at the object's own coordinates — every input needed is in the row.

4 · Clock validation: AG LMi as the standard

an astrophysical event checks the wall clock

Question

Header consistency (sections 1-3) proves the stamps agree with EACH OTHER — not that the acquisition computer's clock agrees with UTC. Does an external astrophysical clock confirm the time axis?

Evidence

AG LMi (type EA, V 10.55 - 11.16) is an eclipsing binary in the archive with P = 1.3590176 d, minimum epoch HJD 2458211.194 (VSX via Vizier B/vsx/vsx, fetched live 2026-08-18). Folding the manifest on this ephemeris finds four nights whose coverage reaches |phase| < 0.02; 192 frames of those plus three baseline nights were measured with forced-aperture differential photometry against 3 fixed comparison stars (176 usable points), using OUR mid-exposure HJD_UTC — the same convention as the VSX epoch, like against like:

Decision

The eclipse arrives at O−C = -411 ± 59 s against the VSX ephemeris; the absolute observatory clock error is bounded at |Δt| < 519 s (< 8.7 min). The offset itself belongs to the EPHEMERIS, not the clock: the independent Gaia DR3 eclipsing-binary period (1.3590118 d, Gaia DR3 vari_eclipsing_binary via Vizier I/358/veb, recorded 2026-08-18) differs from the VSX period by enough to predict an O−C of -763 s at our mean epoch (cycle 1517.0), with a ±3947 s envelope from Gaia's own period uncertainty — our measurement sits comfortably inside that envelope. The bound is an honest ceiling set by how well this star's ephemeris is known, not a claim of NTP-level proof.

Consequence

Every CV timing result inherits this bound as its absolute anchor; relative (within-archive) timing is tighter still, riding on the 0.002 s internal header consistency of section 1. A tighter absolute bound is a one-night project for the October run: one modern AG LMi minimum against a same-week TESS or AAVSO epoch would collapse the ephemeris systematics to seconds.
