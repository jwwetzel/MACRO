1 · The ceiling memo: where every mode's scale actually ends

Question

The SN panel measured the High Gain clip at ~3,530–3,550 ADU; the CV team adopted 3,500 with a 3,200 veto (external priors, quoted from the facility notes as documented constants). Which number is right, is the detector 12-bit, and what are the ceilings of the OTHER seven modes nobody measured?

Method notes, each one load-bearing: (1) the High Gain “clip” is not one code but a 88-ADU-wide pileup mound (per-pixel CMOS saturation spread), peaked at 3,496 and reaching 3,584. Part of that width is egain-epoch drift: the near-ceiling frame maxima split cleanly by camera epoch (egain 1.054: 19 frames, maxima 3,526–3,584 (median 3,557); egain 1.057: 123 frames, maxima 3,262–3,546 (median 3,511)). The SN panel's prior range 3,530–3,550 sits nearest the egain-1.054 epoch's median (3,557), while the CV team's 3,500 sits nearest the egain-1.057 epoch's median (3,511) — the two priors are consistent with having measured different epochs, not different parts of one mound. The adopted 3,496 describes the dominant egain-1.057 population (123 of 142 near-ceiling frames); the veto below sits under every near-ceiling maximum of both epochs (lowest observed: 3,262). (2) a frame-maximum cluster only counts as a ceiling if its members' maxima land at diverse sensor positions (≥50% distinct) — Low Gain's candidate at 2,851 ADU repeated a handful of positions across 55 frames (diversity 0.05) and was rejected, so Low Gain's ceiling is unobserved (bounded below by its hard max 3,546); (3) the 5 MHz iKon mode's steep sky slope was the false positive that forced the two-gate detector (valley-then-mound + nothing-above).

Decision

Adopted per-mode ceilings and vetoes as tabled above: High Gain 3,496 ADU (veto 3,200), StackPro 56,062 (= 16.04 × the single-read clip), Mode0/Fast/blank-2026 at the full 16-bit code 65,535 (veto 60,200), iKon 1 MHz at 64,674. The GSENSE4040 High Gain scale is 12-bit-consistent: the mound at 3,496 fits in 12 bits (212 = 4,096) and leaves 599 codes of the 16-bit FITS container unused — but a per-pixel mound spread over tens of ADU cannot distinguish a 12-bit digitization clip (which lands on ONE shared code, as Mode0/Fast do at 65,535) from per-pixel analog full-well saturation or a clamp below 4,096, so “confirmed” waits for the vendor ADC readback (October). The veto rule is floor(0.92 × ceiling) to 100 ADU; against the CV team's prior veto of 3,200, the measured 3,200 reproduces it exactly.

Consequence

Every downstream photometry pipeline applies the tabled veto for its mode; pixels above it are ceiling-biased, not data. StackPro's 16× headroom is real but its sub-exposures still clip at the single-read mound — a star saturated in High Gain is equally saturated inside every StackPro sub-frame (see §2).

The raw fits above are recorded facts, not adopted gains: the dark slope is sub-Poisson-biased and the light slope motion-biased (both stated on the figure). What the frames DO support cleanly:

  • Read noise — the variance floor of the shortest darks has no scene and only a small dark-current term (1.0% of the floor variance, measured from the 8s-vs-128s floors — negligible but not zero): High Gain 3.93 ADU = 4.15 ± 2.30  e- (value at nominal EGAIN; the electron-unit uncertainty spans the measured gain bracket below, NOT the tiny ADU statistical error); StackPro 15.59 ADU.
  • Gain bracket — High Gain true gain lies between 0.60 (sky-level light slope, lower bound) and 1.77 (dark slope, upper bound) e-/ADU; the header EGAIN 1.057 sits inside the bracket and is adopted as nominal. A clean flat-field PTC is an October item.
  • StackPro architecture — three independent ratios against plain High Gain (offset_ratio=15.96) agree: Nsub = 16. A StackPro frame is the SUM of 16 sub-exposures: bias offset 1,484.5 ADU (≈16 × 93), read-noise variance ≈16× the single read, ceiling ≈16× the single-read clip (§1).
  • Amp glow — High Gain: hottest corner +8.0 ADU (128s dark); a measurable but small spatial term that master darks remove.

Decision

Error models use: read noise 3.93 ADU (High Gain single read); gain = header EGAIN as nominal with the measured [0.60, 1.77] e-/ADU bracket as its systematic; StackPro frames modeled as sums of 16 sub-reads (noise variance 16×, read noise 4× in ADU), NOT as single reads. The repeated darks cannot deliver a full PTC gain — that limit is stated here rather than papered over, and the flat-field PTC joins the October list.

Consequence

Every S/N estimate for StackPro data changes: the effective read noise per frame is 15.59 ADU, sixteen sub-reads' worth, and short-exposure StackPro frames are read-noise-limited far above where a single High Gain read would be.

What that shared content is, the manifest answers: 25,715 of era 79's 25,715 frames — including all 137 in the rawimage tree — are named *_calibrated, and the era's geometry 4,787×3,193 is the CROPPED calibrated-output geometry (era 78's true Fast raws are 4,800×3,211). Decision

The experiment stands: per-pixel line fits recover the reduction's calibration to a few ADU RMS wherever ≥8 clean pairs exist, and the era-47 control grades the method at 5.28 ADU RMS against ground truth (an upper bound — see the degeneracy note above). Reduced-tree photometry is hereby AUDITED for the fitted eras with the table's verdicts attached. Era 79: the 137 rawimage-tree frames are misfiled copies of calibrated products and must NOT be used as raw data (no true raws exist in the archive for that era); its reduced tree stays in service as calibrated data. The reconstructed D/F stamps ship as regenerable products (products/detector/recon/era*.npz) for downstream flat/dark sanity checks.

Consequence

Papers using reduced-tree pixels now cite a measured calibration provenance instead of an assumption; eras with F ≠ 1 are known to be flat-fielded (and by how much, per pixel on the sampled grids); the 2026-era crop offset is on record for anyone aligning raw against reduced frames.

Read the table as single-ladder consistency bounds, not measured non-linearities: each mode's number comes from ONE (night, target, filter, egain) ladder, the median-rate fit zeroes one rung by construction (a 3-rung ladder has two informative rungs), and the residuals fold in sky-transparency drift — which usually inflates the bound but can partially cancel a real roll-off. The rung scatter column is the spread of the clean rungs' residuals, the honest scale of each bound.

Two negative findings, recorded as findings: (1) the Vega ladder is unusable for linearity — its commanded exposure times collapse below ~10 ms (0.0001s → 23,751,922 ADU (peak 40,942); 0.001s → 23,552,458 ADU (peak 36,739); 0.01s → 24,880,892 ADU (peak 43,186); 0.025s → 28,504,038 ADU (peak 48,601): flux is NOT monotonic in commanded time, i.e. the shutter/timing floor, not the detector, dominates); (2) modes with no usable ladder — (blank 2026), 5MHz High Sensitivity 16-bit, Low Gain — have no archival linearity constraint at all. Both go on the October list (a dedicated dome-flat ladder per mode costs minutes).

Decision

Adopted per-mode linearity bounds as tabled: best-witnessed modes (Mode0, Fast, iKon 1 MHz) are linear to ≤1.4% over their witnessed ranges; the High Gain family carries a ≤4.2% bound that is sky/cloud-limited, not detector-limited — treat it as an upper limit, not a measured non-linearity. Sub-10-ms commanded exposure times are untrustworthy on the iKon and must not be used for absolute photometric scaling.

Consequence

Exposure-time-ratio photometry (HDR stitching, ladder bootstraps) inherits the tabled bound as a systematic; the timing-paper error budget cites the sub-10-ms finding directly.
