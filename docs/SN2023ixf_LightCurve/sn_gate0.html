1 · Gate 0a — the manifest freeze

1,129 unique campaign frames from 4,711 catalog rows

Question

How many frames of this supernova does the archive actually hold — once every copy is collapsed and every name the telescope called it by is merged?

Evidence

The strategy's headline inventory is 1,052 unique light frames, obtained by deduplicating the rows whose target field reads 2023ixf. The freeze repeats that dedup globally — across every tree and within rawimage/, where the July directories hold wholesale copies of earlier nights — and then does the thing the strategy's own §3.1 ruling asks for but its headline number does not do: it merges the target's ALIASES. The supernova was observed under NGC5457 on the discovery night and under M101 on the two nights after, and S0's alias table maps both onto the same sky.

So the campaign is 1,129 unique frames, not 1,054 — the difference is the 75 frames that carried a galaxy's name rather than the transient's, and every one of them is a discovery-week epoch. Nothing was double counted to get there: the same global (basename, jd) dedup that produced 1,052 produced this, applied to a target set of two names instead of one.

Two further facts the freeze turned up, both of which change what may be counted:

  • 61 of the campaign's frames are not images at all. They are the slot-'6' frames S2c measures as dispersed. Under the retired filter-label rule all 83 slot-'6' frames would have been called spectra; the measurement says 61 are, 3 are ordinary direct images and 19 could not be certified either way.
  • 2 canonical campaign frames are detector engineering products, not science: mjc/misc/neg10_test/xwg16919.raw.fts.fz, mjc/misc/neg10_test/xwg1691a.raw.fts.fz. They carry the campaign's target name and fall inside its dates. One of them reads 65,535 ADU at the supernova's position — a value the 12-bit High Gain channel cannot physically produce — which is how the census caught them.

The freeze also measures the thing the saturation census needs next: how far the telescope's commanded pointing sits from where it actually lands. Over 749 plate-solved campaign frames the commanded-to-true offset is (-92, -67) arcsec, and the residual scatter about that median is 53 px (median), 108 px (90th percentile), 236 px (worst). That is why an unsolved frame's search box is 250 px and not a guess.

Decision

The campaign is 1,129 unique frames, of which 1,066 are science images. Every number downstream on this page is computed over that set, and the set is a table (sn_g0_frames) rather than a paragraph.

Consequence

The strategy's 1,052 was not wrong about duplication — it was right, and its dedup rule is the one used here. It was incomplete about IDENTITY: a census taken under one of a target's names cannot see the frames filed under the others, and on this target those are exactly the earliest and most contested epochs. The published campaign statistics, and the README the strategy's Step 10 requires be regenerated from the manifest, should be regenerated from sn_g0_frames.

2 · The screen, and where it comes from

clip 3,496 ADU, measured

Question

At what pixel value does this detector stop telling the truth — and does anyone have a measurement of it, or only a guess?

Evidence

When the strategy was written the answer was a guess, and it said so. Its §3.3 quotes a “hard clip at ~3,530–3,550 ADU” and sets a binding screen of 2,800 ADU with a suspect band from 2,400 — numbers derived by eye from an assumed ~3,500 ADU ceiling, because the S2 tables that held the measured one had been destroyed. That is precisely why the project task SN-G0b was marked BLOCKED: a screen with nothing behind it is not a screen.

S2 has since been rebuilt and measures the High Gain channel's ceiling from science-frame histograms. This page therefore stores the strategy's fractions and applies them to the measurement:

quantityvaluesource
measured clip3,496 ADUS2 s2_ceiling_modes.clip_adu, High Gain
S2's own saturation veto3,200 ADUS2 s2_ceiling_modes.veto_adu — an independent cross-check, not used as the screen here
reject screen2,797 ADU (0.80 × clip)the strategy's 80%-of-clip rule, applied to the measurement
suspect floor2,397 ADU (0.686 × clip)the strategy's lower flag level, likewise
The strategy's hand numbers survive contact with the measurement. 0.80 × 3,496 = 2,797 against a typed 2,800, and the suspect floor lands on 2,397 against a typed 2,400 — both inside 0.2%. The assumed ceiling was right. What has changed is that the screen is now a consequence of a query, so an S2 re-measurement moves it without anyone editing a document.

Consequence

Every frame on this sky was screened against the ceiling of the readout mode it was actually taken in, which matters because the post-fade template epochs are not High Gain at all:

A single High Gain screen applied to the 16-bit template epochs would have rejected every one of them; a single 16-bit screen applied to the campaign would have passed every clipped frame in it.

3 · Gate 0b — the saturation census

1,461 frames opened

Question

In which frames is the supernova itself measurable, and in which is it sitting on the detector's ceiling? The strategy's answer was an exposure-scaling argument. This is the pixels.

Evidence

Every frame on this sky was opened read-only and the supernova's catalogue position projected onto it. Where the frame carried its own plate solution the position is exact and the peak of a 21 px stamp is the supernova's peak; where it did not, the position is known only to the pointing residual measured in §1, and the census reports the maximum of a 501 px search box — which is an UPPER BOUND on the supernova's peak and is labelled as one. A frame whose whole box stays under the screen is provably unsaturated wherever the supernova fell in it; a frame whose box reaches the screen supports no conclusion at all, and is recorded as undetermined rather than as a saturated epoch it may not be.

The census validates its own position rule as it goes. In every plate-solved frame it also records the ISOLATION radius — how far away the nearest brighter pixel is — which measures directly how often “the brightest thing in the search box” would have been the wrong source. In the broadband codes that misidentification rate is a few per cent; in the narrowband codes it is an order of magnitude worse, which is why no narrowband bound was ever promoted to a measurement.

Decision

The clean broadband start is night 2023-05-23 (UT 05-24, +5.4 d), in all three broadband codes, exactly where the strategy predicted it by exposure scaling — and now by measurement. The three earlier epochs contain not one clean broadband frame between them: 2023-05-19 (+1.6 d) — 0 clean of 15 frames, 6 over the screen; 2023-05-20 (+2.5 d) — 0 clean of 12 frames, 11 over the screen; 2023-05-21 (+3.5 d) — 0 clean of 15 frames, 15 over the screen. The balance in each is undetermined: frames with no plate solution whose search box reaches the screen, which the census declines to call either way.
Saturation here is a per-frame property, not a per-epoch one. 39 of 104 broadband filter-night cells contain a clean frame AND a clipped frame on the same night in the same filter. The strategy anticipated this in one sentence (§3.3: the supernova reaches 3,222 ADU in a good-seeing 2 s frame on a night when a 0.5 s frame reads 891); the census makes it the rule rather than the anecdote. No epoch may be admitted or excluded wholesale — the screen has to be applied frame by frame, and the released table has to carry the flag per frame.

Consequence

The narrowband result is the one that changes a plan. The strategy names the +5.4 and +6.4 d 32 s frames as “the only certain-clean H epochs” and warns that “there is a live risk that the only unsaturated flash-phase Hα record in this archive is the slitless grism series.” The census disagrees, and it disagrees with a measurement: see §4.

4 · The flash-phase Hα record is not only the grism

+2.5 d, measured clean

Question

Is the slitless series really the archive's only unsaturated flash-phase narrowband record of this supernova?

Evidence

No. On night 2023-05-20 (UT 05-21, +2.5 d) the narrowband exposures were 64 s, and the census measures the supernova at a few hundred to ~1,900 ADU in them — well under the 2,397 ADU flag level. Two independent checks say this is the supernova and not a field star or an H II region that was always there.

  • Position. The clean +2.5 d frames put the peak within 0.1–0.7 px of the catalogue position, with the nearest brighter pixel tens of pixels away.
  • The pre-explosion epoch, which the census also measured. In the 2023-05-05 H frames the same position yields 1.7 net ADU s−1; at +2.5 d it yields 18, and at +5.4 d 31. The source is 10× brighter than whatever occupied that position two weeks earlier.
11 narrowband frames at +2.5 d are measured unsaturated on the supernova — three nights earlier than the strategy's stated clean narrowband start, and they are images, not spectra. The claim that the grism is the only unsaturated flash-phase Hα record in this archive is retracted here, on the evidence of the frames it was made about.

Consequence

This does not by itself resurrect the Hα product. It moves what is blocking it. Q2 was thought to be dead in the flash phase because of saturation; it is not, and the blocker is now the one the strategy already named — the narrowband transmission curve, without which a flux at +2.5 d cannot be interpreted physically (task SN-S1-narrowband-curves). It also means the flash-phase narrowband coverage is three epochs (+2.5, +5.4, +6.4 d), not two.

The pre-explosion template epoch was measured in the same pass, and it settles a caution the strategy raised but could not resolve:

The strategy flagged the 2023-05-05 G/R/X frames as “possibly trailed/defocused” on their 14–16 px FWHM and ruled them narrowband-only by default. The narrowband H/O templates sit at a few per cent and are fine. The strategy's ruling stands, and now has a number under it.

5 · Does the astrometry verdict move?

NO-GO on the stratum

Question

The strategy inherited a NO-GO on this campaign's astrometry from the S1 experiment, whose candidate universe was contaminated by spectra it had excluded by filter LABEL. With the gate repaired to read S2c's per-frame measurement, does the verdict move?

Decision

The verdict does not move: the stratum stays NO-GO. 18/48 = 37.5% on the S1 stratum (unchanged verdict NO-GO); 553/630 = 87.8% of campaign broadband images carry a corroborated solution (GO).
But it is a verdict about long narrowband exposures, not about the gri light curve. Its unsolved population is 217 narrowband frames against 68 broadband, and 18 of its 30 autopsied failures are O-band frames diagnosed as trailed. The light curve's own astrometry is not a blind-batch problem at all: the solutions already exist, and this census independently corroborated them by finding the supernova within 6 px of its catalogue position in each one.

Consequence

Two things follow for the plan. First, the re-solve task (SN-S4-resolve) should be scoped to the bands it can help: attempting the O-band residue blind is what the NO-GO is about, and the strategy's “~177 unsolved broadband frames” is the tractable part. Second, a corroborated plate solution is a stronger claim than a PLTSOLVD card, and this campaign now has one per frame — a by-product of the census that Step 4 can consume instead of re-deriving.

Decision

The series is genuine and it does not promote. 61 measured spectra on 13 nights, 3 of them inside the flash window; 0 extracted spectra. It has the nights — exactly the three the criterion asks for, with no margin — and it has none of the spectra. Consequence

This is a promotion the evidence does not support, and saying so is the result. The strategy pre-registered the criterion precisely so that a real, well-sampled, genuinely interesting set of frames could not be talked into being a headline product before anyone had extracted a spectrum from it. The frames are worth the two-week timebox the strategy allots them; they are not worth a venue decision.

One limitation of this census bears directly on that timebox and should be stated rather than discovered later: not one of the 83 slot-'6' frames carries a plate solution, so Gate 0b could not determine whether the traces themselves are clipped. The 512 s exposures from +9.4 d onward are the ones at risk. The extraction will have to establish its own astrometry — which is exactly what the verified paired direct images above are for.

Decision

The venue posture stays at AJ/PASP. The strategy decided in advance that ApJ is taken only if Gate 0 promotes the grism or recovers the narrowband bandpass. Gate 0 did neither: zero contamination-tested spectra against a bar of 3, and zero recovered transmission curves against a bar of one. A posture that was decided before the evidence arrived, and that the evidence does not move, is a posture that held.

Consequence

What Gate 0 delivers is not a promotion; it is a scope. The paper has 438 broadband frames it may take photometry from, a measured clean start, a per-frame saturation flag for the released table, a corroborated plate solution for most of the light curve, and three narrowband epochs inside the flash phase it did not know it had. Steps 1 and 2 are now unblocked and are the next binding gates.

What Gate 0 does NOT settle, stated so it is not mistakenly assumed: the physical identity of the filter codes (Step 1), the linearity curve below the screen — which is what would let the 46 suspect broadband frames be recovered (Step 2), whether the grism traces are clipped (no plate solutions), and the narrowband transmission profiles without which the Hα product stays a methods demonstration.
