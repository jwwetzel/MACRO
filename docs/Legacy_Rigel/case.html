Legacy Archive 2015–2022 (census only) — the case

A CENSUS, not a paper — and no science commitment before it. This is the pre-MACRO Winer/Iowa archive: about 210,000 files across ~1,100 nights (a file count from the transfer itself — NOT a pipeline-emitted frame count, because nothing here has reached the manifest yet). It was filed as the 'Rigel' archive on the premise that it is a different telescope with one camera; the headers say otherwise. Only the first few per cent, through spring 2015, is the Rigel system with the FLI ProLine PL16803. From late 2015 it is the 0.5 m telescope itself, with six cameras in turn — the last of which, the SBIG Aluma AC4040, is the 2023 RLMT camera. The census (cameras first, then targets) decides whether there is a paper; the go/no-go criteria are pre-registered before the census is seen and must name a question, a reader and a venue, or the outcome is a data-release note.

Where it is going: none — census only (L0–L1); a pre-registered go/no-go, else a data-release note

11 question(s) answered on this project's own evidence pages, 15 inherited from the shared pipeline, 15 still open in the plan.

The census population is the scanned .fz files; everything else on disk or in the manifest is a named exclusion, and both identities have zero residual. The download was made from the corrected manifest; the bad one overwrote nothing.

Q3

The dedup rule

The referee asked that the go/no-go rest on a census with a stated dedup rule. When are two files the same exposure, and which one counts?

The deciding number
one exposure, one canonical frame
The decision it produced
Canonical = raw over calibrated twin, then smallest path; every threshold counts canonical science frames only. Known limit, stated: the key cannot see a copy whose header time was rewritten; none of the software in this archive is known to do that, and the (target, night, exposure) populations show no doubled cadence.

Q6

The header-time convention

Two acquisition systems and nine cameras wrote these headers. Is DATE-OBS UTC? Does it mark the start of the exposure? Is it there at all? And how much of that can a header census establish?

The deciding number
what DATE-OBS means, before any timing is believed
The decision it produced
DATE-OBS is UTC throughout. It is the START of the exposure in 11 camera epochs; it is NOT the start in 4: MIDDLE of exposure — SBIG Aluma AC4040 (2021-11-11 to 2023-07-06); not identifiable from headers (no unequal consecutive exposures) — CCD42-40 camera (model not in header) (2018-02-03 to 2018-02-04). Where the verdict is “middle”, both independent tests say so: every unequal-exposure pair is consistent with a mid-exposure stamp, start and end are each refuted by hundreds of pairs, and the LST residual falls as half the exposure time. Not established, and not establishable from headers: the absolute clock. JD and LST derive from the same computers as DATE-OBS; a PC clock that was a minute slow would pass every test here. The observational astronomer's criterion (O−C of archived minima within 60 s of the literature, per season) needs photometry and is outside a census; section 8b lists the nights that could carry it.

Q8

Overlap with the RLMT-era targets

Were T CrB, the polars, YZ Cnc, the Be stars or the dwarf-survey fields observed here in 2015–2022 — and if so, on enough nights, in a filter that can be tied to the RLMT-era photometry?

The deciding number
the one place a legacy frame can extend a baseline
The decision it produced
The overlap is what the series table says and no more. A series that passes G2 is a candidate baseline extension handed to the owning project; it is conditional on a pixel test the census cannot make (saturation at the target, per frame, in native pixels — standing rule 4) and on the clock caveat of section 5.

Q9

8a · Calibration census, per camera

For each camera: are there bias, dark and flat frames in the archive at all, are there flat pairs so that gain can be measured rather than read from a header, and were the science frames left raw?

The deciding number
frames that exist · flat pairs for a measured gain
The decision it produced
Calibration availability is reported as counted, and no gain is asserted. The pre-registered “calibrated night” (a same-filter flat and a bias or dark on the same camera and binning within ±30 nights, as frames in the archive) is met by at most 0 nights of any one series. Flat pairs exist only where the table shows them; where it shows none, the camera's gain cannot be measured from this archive and the header EGAIN is a manufacturer's number.

Q10

8b · Eclipsing and contact systems

A period change of 10−7 d yr−1 accumulates about seven minutes of O−C in ten years, which one-minute timings detect. How many systems in the archive have eclipse minima observed in at least 3 seasons?

The deciding number
minima in ≥ 3 seasons — the physicist's criterion
The decision it produced
The count the physicist asked for is 4 systems with minimum-bearing nights in ≥ 3 seasons. It is a count of nights on which a minimum should be in the data; no minimum has been measured, no light curve inspected, and the clause-(b) nights inherit the catalogue ephemeris error.

The pre-registered rule, applied

The deciding number
NOT DECIDABLE — fix the census
The decision it produced
Outcome as written: NOT DECIDABLE — fix the census.
The rule cannot be applied yet: a validity clause fails or an input table is absent. Nothing below is a go.
Amended reading (a deviation, reported beside the written one as pre-registration §5 requires): GO-CANDIDATE. G0 fails only because camera epochs that feed G1 do not stamp the exposure start (SBIG Aluma AC4040 / MaxIm DL 6.29; SBIG Aluma AC4040 / MaxIm DL 6.30). “Fix the census” has an obvious meaning here: drop the runs from those epochs and re-evaluate. Row G1* does that; systems still passing: HS0705+6700, HS2231+2441, HW Vir, NY Vir. Under that reading rule 4 applies — GO-CANDIDATE, with the named question, reader and venue of Q1 (period change from eclipse timings; ephemeris maintainers and eclipse-timing observers; JAAVSO/OEJV class). It is a recommendation to commission a strategy, not a commitment; the decision is James's and must not compete with T CrB this autumn. Before any strategy is written, a literature and novelty check on the passing systems comes first (the rule the committee set for the Be stars, U9): eclipse timing of short-period eclipsing binaries is an active field, and nights in this archive may already be published.
