## Trigger

Either of:

- an AAVSO Alert Notice, ATel or CBET reporting T CrB in eruption; or
- any reliable measurement at least 1.0 mag brighter than quiescence (V ≈ 10) that is
  confirmed by a second measurement at least 10 minutes later.

One unconfirmed bright point is not a trigger. Do not wait for us: if the trigger is met
and the star is reachable tonight, start.

## Which block

The 1946 eruption rose to V ≈ 2 in hours, fell by about three magnitudes in the first
week, was back near V ≈ 9–10 within about a month, and then produced a second, broad
maximum some three to four months after the first. Choose the block by the latest
reported V magnitude:

| Latest V | Block | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| brighter than 4 | 1 — peak | every hour while reachable |
| 4 to 7 | 2 — early decline | every two hours while reachable |
| fainter than 7 | 3 — late decline | once per night; twice during the secondary maximum |
| unknown | 1 first, then 2 | — |

If in doubt between two blocks, run both: they take a few minutes each.

Nobody has observed T CrB in eruption with this camera, so the exposures are predicted:
the trace peak of the quiescent star measured in the 2025 frames, carried to the QHY600
through stars both cameras observed, and scaled by the V-band brightening
(request rev. 3, §6.A and §7). The eruption spectrum will be hotter than the quiescent
M giant and its Hα will not scale with V, so each prediction is bracketed by a factor of
four either side. **The intent is that at least one rung per grism is well exposed and
none of the science depends on which.** Saturated rungs are expected and harmless.

- **No direct imaging while the star is brighter than V ≈ 7.** It saturates at any
  exposure the camera can time reliably. The grisms are the attenuator.
- **Do not change the gain, the offset, the binning or the set-point** for the eruption,
  and do not refocus the grisms. Homogeneity with the quiescent series is the whole value
  of these data.
- The g, r, i ladders of block 3 are computed for the mild defocus of the nightly block
  (request §6.A). If the telescope is in focus the star is about three times brighter per
  pixel and the shorter rungs are the well-exposed ones; either way the ladder covers it.
- Exposures below about 0.1 s rely on the camera's electronic shutter timing, which has
  not been verified on this camera; the 0.05 s floor of the ladders reflects that. The
  frames remain useful for line profiles even if their absolute exposure is uncertain.

## Standards (every eruption night)

Immediately after the T CrB block, through both grisms, without refocusing:

- θ CrB, 60 s hrg and 15 s lrg (the 2026 comparison);
- BD+33°2642, 240 s hrg and 240 s lrg (flux standard at the quiescent star's brightness);
- Vega, 4 s hrg and 0.25 s lrg, if it is up.

and a bright sky background: take them anyway, and take the standards at the same
airmass.
